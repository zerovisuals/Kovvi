# Freelancer acquisition product — complete working brief

Prepared 13 September 2026. Updated naming direction: short abstract master brand, similar in spirit to Koto. Final shortlist: Otevu / Noketu. Name selection remains open; the older compound-name shortlist below is historical and superseded by section 9.

## 1. Shared direction

Build a multi-niche subscription application that helps skilled freelance website designers find relevant businesses, investigate their needs, prepare evidence-based approaches, and manage the resulting sales conversations. Launch with multiple industry modules sharing one research and sales workflow. Esports is one use case, not the product's identity or total addressable audience.

Founder context: experienced designer/developer with shipped KDS and Kyviera esports websites; two clients won from five Discord contacts. This establishes personal experience of the workflow, not a reliable market conversion rate. The founder assumes a complete seven-day build, including one day of branding and approximately three days of coding. This brief accepts that schedule and defines what finished means. Operating commitment is approximately 20 hours weekly. Pro price is €65/month. Target is an enjoyable, manageable side business capable of recurring income.

### Product promise

Emotional campaign territory: “Heaven for freelancers.” Functional promise: “Discover organizations that fit your work. Get the evidence, contact and approach prepared.” When approved email execution is operational, supporting copy can say “Your prospecting and approved follow-ups, handled.” Do not say customers arrive without outreach: outbound remains outbound even when software performs it. A lead is not a client, an observed issue is not demonstrated revenue loss, and an active organization is not necessarily a funded buyer.

### Users and customers

- Primary paying customer: an independent web designer/developer with credible work, a defined service and capacity for new projects who dislikes prospecting.
- Initial prospect industries: fashion/ecommerce, creators, hospitality, local services, professional services, software/startups and esports. Users can work across several industries.
- Secondary paying customer: a small creative studio with repeated prospecting needs.
- Expansion customers: branding, video and other creative-service freelancers, once assessments support those services rather than simply reusing website checks.
- Poor initial fit: users expecting guaranteed income, generic bulk-email operators, or beginners with no service to sell.

### Core job

“When I have space for a project, help me discover organizations I can credibly help, understand why they might buy now, and begin a relevant conversation without hours of searching.”

### Distinctive hypothesis

The opportunity is portfolio-aware prospecting with industry-specific research: understand each business model, resolve identities across sources, interpret relevant commercial events, match the freelancer's work, and carry evidence into the pitch. A fashion store needs a different assessment from a consultancy or esports organization. General website audits and AI-written outreach already exist. Neither uniqueness nor superior accuracy has been established yet.

## 2. End-to-end experience and functional scope

1. User adds portfolio URLs, two representative projects, services, minimum project price, preferred regions, supported contact languages and excluded organizations. They confirm extracted claims before use.
2. User chooses one or more industries, regions, business maturity and current capacity. They can add past clients as similarity examples without granting permission to disclose private information. Industry filters appear conditionally; users never have to understand esports terminology to use the product.
3. System discovers candidates from supported public sources and shows the source coverage. Missing source access is visible.
4. It resolves businesses, brands, parent entities and domains; assesses public commercial activity; inspects confirmed websites; identifies dated opportunities and public contacts.
5. User reviews a ranked shortlist with proof, dismisses unsuitable results and corrects mistaken matches.
6. System drafts an approach grounded in the opportunity and a relevant portfolio project.
7. User copies a Discord/social message or approves a supported email campaign. The UI labels each channel’s capabilities accurately.
8. Replies and manual outcomes feed a lightweight pipeline. User handles negotiation, contractual commitments and final commercial decisions.
9. Saved searches bring fresh events, exclude previous contacts and report meaningful changes.

### Launch requirements

**Profile and matching:** editable portfolio extraction; user-confirmed skills and project role; services, price floor, location, language and exclusions; explain why a project supports a match. Language is a communication preference, not a translation business feature.

**Multi-niche discovery:** supported public sources, dated announcements, business directories and manual URL import. Each module declares its filters, evidence rules and coverage. Record parent businesses to avoid approaching branches, sub-brands or rosters as unrelated buyers. A directory entry or social account alone does not establish an active business.

### Launch industry modules

The following source types are implementation targets, subject to verified access. They are not claims that integrations already exist. All modules use the same evidence, identity and outreach contracts.

| Module | Discovery sources | Reasons to investigate | Assessment and fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fashion and ecommerce | Brand sites, public launch announcements, stockist directories, accessible social/shop links | Collection launch, new wholesale channel, social-only sales, rebrand | Mobile product discovery, size information, shop continuity; match commerce and brand projects |
| Creators and independent media | Public creator profiles, channel descriptions, newsletters, product announcements | Course/product launch, sponsorship activity, scattered commercial links | Offer clarity, landing pages and owned destination; subscribers alone do not prove budget |
| Hospitality and venues | Official venue sites, tourism directories, local openings and accessible listings | New opening/location, event program, rebrand | Menu, booking and event information on mobile; do not classify a functioning external booking platform as a defect |
| Local services | Business directories, official pages, opening announcements and geographic search | Expansion, new service, inaccessible quote/contact path | Service-area clarity, contact and booking; verify domains beyond directory fields |
| Professional services | Business/association directories, company sites and public announcements | New practice, specialization, team growth or rebrand | Expertise, credibility and inquiry path; do not infer confidential client or financial details |
| Software and startups | Public launch directories, product sites, company announcements | Product launch, new positioning, funding announcement | Product explanation, demo/signup path and brand consistency; funding is context, not a promised website budget |
| Esports and gaming organizations | Tournament/team sources and official roster, sponsor or merchandise announcements | Team expansion, jersey launch, rebrand | Parent organization identity, roster/partner/shop presentation; distinguish an independent squad from a funded organization |

Support cross-niche searches and separate module-level performance reporting. A custom brief may search the public web with generic checks, but must not pretend to provide specialized coverage that has not been built. If a source fails, show partial coverage rather than manufacturing results.

**Identity verification:** inspect candidate domains and official cross-links. Distinguish confirmed official site, probable site, conflicting identities, inaccessible site and no site found after recorded searches. Never convert a missing directory field into confirmed absence. Preserve aliases, rebrands and historical domains.

**Website inspection:** desktop/mobile captures, working navigation, visible purchase/contact routes, status/redirect checks and dated findings. Avoid sending test contact forms or placing orders. Classify objective defects, subjective presentation observations and commercial hypotheses separately. A blocked browser test is inconclusive, not a broken website. A footer date alone cannot establish abandonment.

**Opportunity evidence:** record event date separately from discovery date; source URL, short supporting excerpt, retrieved time and confidence. Examples: a collection launch with an inaccessible shop link; a venue opening with no working booking route; a consultancy rebrand with inconsistent public pages; a jersey drop with outdated merchandise information. These are sample scenarios, not claims about real named businesses.

**Contacts:** publicly listed role, channel and source; verified/probable status; last checked date. Do not invent addresses, imply ownership from a nickname or assume a public Discord invite authorizes automated direct messages.

**Ranking:** show fit, opportunity evidence, timing, activity and contactability separately. Initial internal ranking may weight these 30/25/20/15/10 respectively; these are proposed heuristics, not calibrated purchase probabilities. Identity ambiguity blocks campaign eligibility regardless of score. Missing evidence remains unknown. Present High/Medium/Low confidence rather than false precision.

**Outreach:** short factual drafts, selectable portfolio reference, tone editing, user-approved recipient/message set, scheduling and supported follow-ups. Any material change to an approved message requires approval again. Stop on replies, opt-outs, bounces and manual holds. Do not claim guaranteed revenue improvements or offer unauthorized discounts.

**Pipeline:** discovered, shortlisted, prepared, approved, contacted, replied, qualified, quoted, won, lost and suppressed. Keep research/job statuses separate from sales stages. Capture loss reasons and optional deal value. Manual channels use manual contact logging.

**Monitoring:** configurable refreshes; new-to-user and genuinely new business/event distinguished; no stale-event notifications; suppress duplicates; pause when user is booked. No exclusivity claim unless allocation is actually enforced.

**Account operations:** authentication, billing, cancellation, usage ledger, export, deletion, support route, understandable errors and recoverable job state.

### Deliberately later

Additional industry specializations beyond the launch modules, additional automatic sending integrations, proposal/e-signature/payment collection, a full CRM, advanced team roles, automatic website mockup generation and cross-user exclusivity allocation. These are expansion scope; the launch workflow must work end to end across multiple industries in its explicitly supported channels.

## 3. Designer brief

### Desired feeling

Calm, capable and unusually useful. Users should feel that tedious work has already been done and that the evidence is easy to inspect. The design should appeal to designers through typography, pacing and clarity. Avoid casino-like lead counters, aggressive sales imagery and decorative complexity that slows review.

### Brand system

Create wordmark, small-size symbol, monochrome versions, color and typography tokens, icon rules, motion principles and source-credit styling. Use a short abstract master brand with clear pronunciation, rounded rhythm and strong typographic presence, taking Koto as a tonal reference without copying its identity. Avoid literal lead/client/pitch/brief compounds. Explain the function through supporting copy and demonstrations. The identity serves freelancers across industries from launch. Show a balanced mix of prospect industries in demos; gaming imagery belongs only in relevant prospect content.

Marketing may use expressive motion and rich project imagery. The application prioritizes fast scanning, legible evidence and restrained transitions. Respect reduced motion. Provide strong contrast, keyboard focus, readable body text and non-color status indicators.

### Navigation

Today / Discover / Shortlist / Outreach / Conversations / Pipeline. Profile, saved searches, connections, billing and preferences live under settings. Today contains opportunities needing review, replies needing attention and research progress—not vanity analytics.

### Screen specifications

1. **Landing:** functional promise immediately below headline; real recorded workflow; annotated sample opportunity; supported channels; founder’s relevant work clearly labeled; price and allowance; FAQ explaining what remains manual. No fabricated testimonials or revenue counters.
2. **Onboarding:** portfolio → service/price → industry/region → confirm extracted profile → first research run. Connection to email is optional until sending is requested. Show sample data as sample data.
3. **Research setup:** industry multi-select, regions, business maturity, source coverage, freshness and exclusions; conditional module-specific filters; cost estimate and bounded search limit before starting.
4. **Results:** compact rows containing business, industry/region, reason now, portfolio match, evidence confidence, verified website state and next action. Filters for industry, freshness, contactability, stage and issue type. Save/dismiss keyboard actions. Explain why two differently scored industries should not be compared using raw diagnostic counts.
5. **Opportunity detail:** organization summary, original source links, dated event, website screenshots, contrary evidence, contact provenance, relevant portfolio work and pitch. Source inspection must be within one interaction of a claim.
6. **Campaign review:** recipient, message, channel, follow-up schedule, exclusions and projected usage. Bulk approval must show the actual batch, not an opaque “autopilot” toggle.
7. **Conversation:** thread, organization context, draft assistance and next step. Do not automatically label polite replies as buying intent.
8. **Pipeline:** accessible list first; optional board. Dates, next action, quoted value and manual outcome capture.
9. **Billing/usage:** available allowance, completed research, failed-job refunds, renewal/cancellation and export. No surprise overspend.

### Required states

Loading with honest stage progress; no matches; partial results; source unavailable; identity conflict; unknown website; no contact; expired evidence; duplicate; research capped; account disconnected; paused campaign; failed send; opted out; subscription inactive. Show recovery actions and preserve useful completed work.

### Designer handoff

Deliver component library, responsive desktop/mobile layouts, clickable core flow, all error/empty states, motion specifications, microcopy, editable source files and exportable brand assets. Annotate factual versus inferred information. Complete a usability walkthrough: a new user must explain why a lead is suitable, locate the source, correct a mismatch and prepare a message without coaching.

## 4. Developer brief

### Architecture

Use a conventional web application, relational database, object storage for bounded captures, background jobs and isolated browser workers. Separate discovery, enrichment, assessment and sending. A job orchestrator records each stage, retries recoverable failures and exposes progress. Choose specific vendors after checking current pricing, access terms and operational fit; this brief does not prescribe unverified APIs.

### Domain model

- Workspace/User: access, preferences, plan and account state.
- ServiceProfile/PortfolioProject: user-confirmed capabilities, project role and public references.
- Business/Brand/Location: normalized identities and parent relationships; optional module-specific entities such as Roster/Competition.
- NicheModule/SourceAdapter/AssessmentRule: versioned discovery configuration, conditional filters, evidence rules and test cases for each industry.
- SourceRecord/Evidence/Event: provenance, observed/published/retrieved times and factual claims.
- WebsiteCandidate/Assessment: identity status, captures, checks, findings and model version.
- Contact: role, channel, source and verification status.
- Opportunity: workspace-specific fit, explanations, exclusion and sales stage.
- ResearchRun/Job: budget, stage, provider calls, retries and terminal status.
- Campaign/Message/Approval: content version, authorized audience, schedule and send status.
- Conversation/Outcome: replies, manual activity, quote and won/lost outcome.
- Suppression/UsageLedger/Subscription/AuditEvent: operational controls and financial accounting.

All private records are tenant-scoped. Shared public evidence must never expose another user’s messages, customers, prices or outcomes.

### Processing contract

Discover → normalize → resolve identity → collect evidence → inspect confirmed domains → identify contacts → rank → draft → user review → optional supported send → track outcome. Each stage uses typed validated outputs. Store source references at claim level. An LLM summary cannot become its own evidence. Treat fetched web content as untrusted data, including instructions embedded in pages.

Website resolution should compare official links, brand names, regional context and organization ownership rather than relying on domain similarity alone. Search failure, bot blocking and unknown identity must propagate as uncertainty. Rebranded or dissolved organizations require history rather than destructive merging.

### Integration contracts

Each source adapter declares permissions/access method, supported entity types, freshness, pagination, rate limits and failure behavior. Start with accessible public web discovery and supported data providers. Do not depend on an unverified private Discord feed or universal social scraping capability.

Implement one supported email integration for launch if automatic sending is promised. Use provider authorization and webhook/polling recovery; encrypt tokens and restrict scopes. Discord/social contact can be a prepared-message handoff with explicit manual tracking. Do not implement self-bot account automation. Channel capabilities must match the product UI.

### Reliability and security

Use idempotency keys for sends, billing and job retries. Check suppression immediately before dispatch. A reply arriving during scheduling must cancel subsequent sends. Handle webhook duplication and out-of-order events. Pause sends when token access fails. Keep a user-visible audit history.

Browser workers must block private/local network targets, cloud metadata endpoints and unsafe redirects; defend against SSRF and malicious downloads. Sanitize fetched content and CSV exports. Limit file size, crawl depth, time and redirects. Do not execute arbitrary prospect code outside isolated browsers. Use server-side authorization and tenant-isolation tests. Limit secrets in logs; provide retention/deletion controls and backup recovery.

### Budget accounting

Separate cheap discovery from expensive verification. Reserve credits before deep work; finalize only once; refund failed paid units. Cached repeat results should not silently count as fresh research. Track cost per run, reviewed opportunity and active subscriber. Apply hard per-run limits and provider circuit breakers. Heavy screenshots and enrichment must not run unbounded.

### Acceptance tests

1. Missing directory site but a real official domain: find or mark uncertain, never assert absence.
2. Two organizations with the same name: prevent silent merge and sending until resolved.
3. One parent with multiple branches, brands or rosters: shared ownership context and appropriate business contact deduplication.
4. Blocked website inspection: inconclusive assessment, no fabricated defects.
5. Old announcement discovered today: preserve its actual event age.
6. Source says ignore instructions: ignore that instruction and continue evidence extraction.
7. Timeout/retry/webhook replay: no duplicate charge or send.
8. Reply or opt-out before follow-up: follow-up canceled.
9. Tenant A requests tenant B’s evidence containing private notes: access denied.
10. Budget exhausted mid-run: retain partial results and stop cleanly.
11. Expired campaign evidence: request refresh before new outreach.
12. Export/delete/cancel: verified end-to-end account operation.
13. Multi-industry search: correct sources and filters for each selected module; no game-specific assumptions applied to other businesses.
14. External shop or booking provider: recognize a valid commercial journey instead of claiming the business lacks one.
15. Cross-module duplicate: the same apparel/esports business is one prospect, not charged twice for the same completed assessment.

### Definition of done

A user can register, pay, submit their profile, research multiple supported industry segments, inspect evidence, correct a lead, prepare outreach, use the supported sending/manual path, record replies/outcomes, receive a fresh search result, inspect charges and cancel/export. Each advertised launch module passes its own benchmark cases. No critical tenant-isolation, billing or duplicate-send failures. Operational monitoring and support documentation exist. Unsupported integrations are explicitly absent rather than simulated.

## 5. Director / CEO / management brief

### Commercial thesis and competitive position

Sell reduced prospecting effort and better-fit opportunities at €65/month to website freelancers across multiple industries. Do not sell guaranteed clients. Existing products already cover substantial parts of the generic workflow; the bet is that portfolio matching plus several well-designed industry research modules is more useful than generic local-business scraping. Its defensibility would come from resolved identities, evaluated industry sources, customer feedback and workflow quality—not a long feature list alone. Price the shared service rather than charging users again simply to switch industries.

### Pricing proposal

One Pro plan at €65/month. Suggested starting allowance for costing: 100 completed deep organization assessments, three saved searches and one connected email account. These are provisional limits, not a commitment before measuring actual cost. Display whether refreshes consume allowance. Failed jobs do not consume completed-assessment credits. Offer a small sample batch for evaluation and transparent cancellation; introduce annual billing only after renewal value is established.

16 subscribers × €65 = €1,040; 31 × €65 = €2,015. These are gross subscription billings, not take-home income; tax treatment, payment fees, data, inference, infrastructure, refunds and support reduce the result. Model revenue net of applicable tax. If net revenue per account were €65, a 75% contribution-margin target would allow €16.25 of variable costs; this is an internal target, not an observed margin. Set allowance from measured costs, including expensive tail users.

### Launch and operating timeline

- Day 1: finalize brand, product states, source feasibility and benchmark cases for every launch industry.
- Days 2–4: implement discovery through evidence, outreach, tracking and account foundations.
- Day 5: integrate the complete journey, instrument costs and test real source failures.
- Day 6: verify acceptance cases and observe initial users; fix material issues.
- Day 7: launch the supported product with real demonstration data, billing and support.
- Weeks 2–3: observe 5–10 qualified freelancers across at least three different target industries using their own portfolios and targets.
- Weeks 4–6: ask for full-price payment, compare with users’ current research methods and inspect actual conversations.
- Months 2–3: evaluate renewals, source exhaustion, duplicate exposure and support burden by industry before adding further specializations.

This is a work allocation under the founder’s assumed build speed, not a guarantee of third-party approvals or customer acquisition.

### Distribution experiment

Recruit initial testers through the founder’s relevant design contacts and communities where product sharing is permitted. Demonstrate one real discovery-to-evidence workflow and offer a sample generated for the designer’s own portfolio. Do not depend on viral reach or mass unsolicited messages. Show anonymized or permissioned examples; do not expose private client conversations. Record where each trial and paying account came from. The founder’s own website sales are a separate use case and must not be confused with SaaS subscription demand.

### Decision metrics

Primary: customers who repeatedly use a researched opportunity to initiate or progress a qualified sales conversation. Track activation, opportunity acceptance, time saved, public-contact accuracy, research cost, positive replies, quotes, optional wins, renewal and support minutes. Define activation as profile confirmed plus three opportunities reviewed plus one saved or prepared approach. Do not use open rates or total scraped rows as proof of value.

Proposed internal gates, not external benchmarks: ≥90% identity accuracy on 50 manually checked cases; ≥50% top-20 opportunity acceptance by target users; median useful-shortlist time at least halved against their existing method; five full-price customers by the end of a deliberate six-week sales test. Missing the payment target triggers investigation of distribution versus product value, not an automatic shutdown. Observe two renewal cycles before calling retention proven.

### Main risks and actions

**Crowded concept:** compare output quality on the same task; avoid claiming generic audit uniqueness. **Small niche:** measure new qualified organizations per user per month and shared-list overlap. **Freelancers pause when booked:** permit pause and measure episodic demand; test batch purchases if subscriptions do not retain. **Budget uncertainty:** preserve unknowns; do not equate sponsors with cash. **Poor sources:** track provider coverage, failures and maintenance time. **Cost overruns:** meter expensive steps and cap usage. **Support overhead:** reserve a fixed weekly operating budget and automate recurring failure explanations. **Outreach requirements:** maintain a documented channel/country operating policy reviewed before sending is offered; public contact availability alone is not universal permission. **Payments/account contracting:** verify provider eligibility for the founder’s age and operating entity before launch.

### Ownership and weekly review

Founder/director owns promise, market choice, pricing and launch gates. Design owns clarity and usability. Engineering owns evidence integrity, reliable execution, security and cost controls. Management owns support, billing disputes and source/provider continuity. A solo founder can hold all roles but should review them separately. Weekly: inspect ten results, all send failures, support themes, variable cost, accepted leads and paying-user activity. Expand only where user demand and source access both justify it.

Portfolio-aware fit, specialized nonlocal sources, identity accuracy and useful campaign execution are the proposed differentiation, not proven exclusive capabilities. Before claiming superiority, run equivalent tasks across at least three industries through accessible competitors and compare identity errors, opportunity acceptance, contact quality, effort and price. This paid/hands-on comparison has not been performed. Most of those projects, though, stated by the client, are "low quality AI slop websites", and no inspiration for our, big project.

## 8. Final launch decision checklist

- Choose between the updated abstract-name candidates after reviewing the checks in section 9; do not continue developing the discarded compound-name direction.
- Confirm supported data sources and channel execution before marketing them.
- Finish all core paths and critical failure states against the definition of done.
- Price allowance against actual costs; clarify tax treatment and credit semantics.
- Demonstrate real research results, with sample data labeled.
- Acquire paying users and evaluate repeat usefulness; do not mistake build completion, compliments or one freelance sale for subscription-market validation.

## 9. Updated naming shortlist and live domain checks

User direction: short, rhythmic and abstract like Koto, rather than a literal description of the product. The earlier five compound names are discarded. The subsequent Tovi/Oru/Noka/Senu/Talo/Kova/Yono/Runo shortlist was rejected because of existing brand/software collisions. In particular Talo and Runo overlap with customer acquisition or sales software.

Final pick: Kovvi.
